Immigration made easyHyderabad, Narita, likely questions, customs

Four short checks, each calm and routine. Keep every document in one folder, and let Harini Manasa speak for the group.

Leaving Hyderabad (RGIA)

Emigration: passport + boarding pass.

Asked purpose, how long, who with? "Tourism, 7 days, family group."

Arriving at Narita T2

  • Indian passports need a Japan visa / eVISA: show it with the passport.
  • Fill in Visit Japan Web before flying for the immigration and customs QR codes; one account can register family members. Otherwise, fill in the paper landing card and customs form on the plane.
  • Fingerprints and a face photo at the desk.
  • Keep the printed return ticket (MH 89, 2 Dec) and the hotel addresses handy.

Visa rules: check the latest at mofa.go.jp.

Likely questions

  • Purpose of visit? Sightseeing / tourism.
  • How many days? 7 days, leaving 2 December.
  • Where are you staying? Show the hotel list: Ryogoku, Kawaguchiko, Kyoto, Narita (Stays).
  • Travelling with? Family, 5 of us.
  • Return ticket? Show it.
  • Money for the trip? Cards and some yen.
  • Carrying any food? Yes, packaged vegetarian ready meals and snacks. Declare them; no meat, fresh fruit, vegetables or seeds.
  • Medicines? Personal prescription medicines with a doctor's note, in original packs. Check strong painkillers and stimulant ingredients are allowed.

Customs

  • Declare honestly; duty-free limits apply.
  • Cash over ¥1,000,000 equivalent must be declared.

As a family

  • Stay together in the same queue.
  • One person, Harini Manasa, speaks for the group.
  • All documents in one folder.
  • Wheelchair assistance can be requested via Malaysia Airlines ahead.

Going home

  • Narita: tax-free refund kiosk before bag drop (how it works), then the face-scan gate at departure immigration.
  • RGIA Hyderabad: immigration, then the customs green channel, unless over India's duty-free allowance.

Money, yen converter & tax-freeCash, cards, ATMs, the tax refund

Mix cash and cards: temples and small shops want yen, everything else takes cards. No tipping in Japan.

¥1 = ₹0.587

⇄

Type in either box. Default rate ¥1 = ₹0.587; change it to your forex card's rate.

Before leaving India

Buy some yen and/or load a multi-currency forex card from an RBI-authorised dealer in Hyderabad, such as Thomas Cook, BookMyForex or your bank.

Carry about ¥20,000–30,000 cash per person.

On arrival

Exchange counters are in the Narita Terminal 2 arrivals hall. Airport rates are usually a little weaker, so change only what you need.

Best in Japan: ATMs

7-Eleven (Seven Bank) and Japan Post Bank ATMs take Visa, Mastercard, RuPay-international and forex cards; many are open 24 hours. Withdraw larger amounts at once to save on fees.

Cards and coins

Cards and Suica/PASMO on your phone work in hotels, big stores and most restaurants. Temples like Todai-ji are cash only, and many shrines, lockers and vending machines take coins.

Tax-free, new rules from 1 Nov 2026

How the refund works

  1. At tax-free shops, pay the full price including tax. Minimum ¥5,000 before tax, per shop per day; show your passport at the till.
  2. At Narita, before checking in bags, scan your passport at a tax-refund kiosk (or use Visit Japan Web, which Narita supports) with all the items in hand.
  3. After the customs check, the 10% tax comes back from the shop or its refund agent by card, app or cash, depending on the shop. Ask each shop how it pays.

Items used up in Japan lose the refund. The kiosk comes first on the journey home.

Back in India

Check India's customs duty-free allowance before bringing back expensive electronics; declare them if over it.

Sources: Japan Customs · Japan Tourism Agency
